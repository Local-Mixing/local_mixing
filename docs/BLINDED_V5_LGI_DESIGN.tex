\noindent\emph{Blinded-V5 is a drop-in alternative to the drip \doc{route\_fire}
compute: it takes a reversible circuit $A$ on $n$ wires (the sliced sandwich)
and emits an equivalent circuit $A'$ on $2n$ wires (data $0..n$, band $n..2n$)
whose body is a shuffled cloud of long masking identities with $A$'s gates
threaded through it. Everything else in the 5-step gadgetization is unchanged.
Source: \doc{src/preprocessing/blinded\_v5.rs}. The Markdown twin of this
document is \doc{BLINDED\_V5\_LGI\_DESIGN.md}.}

\section{Why this design --- the rationale}

The compute stage must move the circuit's intermediate state as far as possible
from the plaintext computation $A$, while keeping $A'$ exactly equivalent to $A$
on the data wires. The design is a chain of forced moves.

\paragraph{(a) Long identities are the only thing that moves the state
substantially.} Across the HD (Hamming-distance) and affine heatmaps, short
local edits barely displace the state: the ridge of recoverable structure only
recedes when the masking identity woven through a wire is \emph{long} ---
comparable to the circuit itself. Short masks are peeled off locally; a long
identity has no local seam to peel.

\paragraph{(b) The only long identity we know that actually moves the state is
the commutative structure of an identity with a single active wire.} Take one
data (``active'') wire $w$ and a set of control wires; a sequence of gates that
all target $w$ and read only the controls is an identity iff the per-gate
increments \textsc{xor} to zero, and --- crucially --- \emph{all of its gates
commute} (same target, disjoint from the controls' evolution). Commutativity is
what lets the identity be arbitrarily long and be reordered or interleaved
freely without changing its value. A single active wire is what makes the whole
run a clean identity on that wire.

\paragraph{(c) We entangle identities on different active wires by separating
the control wires from the active wires, then sharing the same control wires
across many identities at once --- again using commutativity.} Each data wire
$w$ gets its own family of single-active-wire identities, but they all draw
their controls from a common \textbf{band} ($n..2n$). Because every identity
targets a data wire and reads only band wires, identities on \emph{different}
active wires also commute with each other. The scaffold is therefore one large
commuting cloud: many long identities, on many active wires, sharing controls,
interleaved arbitrarily. The shared controls are what ``entangle'' the
identities --- a band wire's value participates in the masks of many data wires
simultaneously.

\paragraph{(d) After entangling by commutation, we add random updates of the
control wires from the active wires.} A pure commuting cloud has a weakness: an
adversary can commute the identities back apart, because nothing couples the
control wires to time or to the data. So we sprinkle in gates that \emph{update}
band wires, half controlled by live data wires ($b \mathrel{\xor}= \mathit{data}
\land \mathit{aux}$) and half by other band wires ($b \mathrel{\xor}=
\mathit{aux} \land \mathit{aux}$). These updates serve two purposes at once:
\begin{enumerate}
\item they \textbf{re-randomize} the control wires (fresh band entropy
  throughout the run, not just at the seed); and
\item they \textbf{break naive commutation-back}: once a band wire has been
  updated mid-run, the identities that read it before and after the update no
  longer commute past it, so the cloud cannot be trivially un-shuffled ---
  \emph{without ruining correctness}, because each identity is repaired across
  the update (\S\ref{sec:construction}, \textsc{repair}) or closed before it
  (\textsc{straddle}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(e) Finally we build one identity longer than the entire input
circuit and embed $A$ inside it, using a hidden unmasking of the control wires.}
The scaffold above is, in effect, a single enormous \lgi{} spread over all
wires. We thread $A$'s gates through it so that each gate fires \emph{from
inside the mask}: at each gate we momentarily and reversibly \textbf{unmask} its
operands into a linear combination of band wires (never the bare operand), fire
the gate as an expansion over those masked wires, and re-mask. The circuit is
embedded inside a single very long identity: \textbf{when an $A$-gate fires, its
operands are read only as linear combinations of band wires --- never bare}
(measured 0.000\% bare reads, \S4). This is a fire-time property of the
operands; the residual plaintext a \emph{global} adversary can still recover is
the public I/O fringe of \S4.2 (input $x$ and the delivered $C(x)$), not an
artifact of the embedding.

\section{The construction}\label{sec:construction}

$A$ has $n$ wires and $m$ gates. $A'$ has $2n$ wires. Let $u_w$ be the number of
times wire $w$ is used (target or control) in $A$.

\paragraph{Step 0 --- band seed (separate module).} Each band wire is seeded
$x_i \land \lnot x_j$ from the honest active inputs by \doc{seed\_band},
pipelined in front of the compute. The compute does \textbf{not} seed the band
(module 0 does); it reads the band as \lgi{} controls and re-randomises it in
place via the Step-3 updates.

\paragraph{Step 1 --- \lgi{} scaffold.} For each data wire $w$, build $u_w + 1$
identities. Each identity is a set of $K$ band wires (``the \lgi's controls'')
realised as $\lfloor K/2 \rfloor$ \textbf{disjoint \g{} pairs}
$\g(w, r_{2i}, r_{2i+1})$, where $\g(w,x,y) = w \mathrel{\xor}= 1 \xor (\lnot x
\land y)$ (comp $=1$). Applying the pair once and removing it once is the
identity; the mask it carries between apply and remove is a disjoint degree-2
form $\bigoplus_i (1 \xor \lnot r_{2i} \land r_{2i+1})$ --- the optimal
sparse-mask shape. All $\approx K(3m+n)$ scaffold gates commute (data targets,
band controls).

\emph{Why disjoint pairs, not a $K$-cycle:} a \g{} gate and its reverse
\textbf{linearise}, $\g(w,r_1,r_2) \xor \g(w,r_2,r_1) = w \xor r_1 \xor r_2$
(\textsc{and} monomials are symmetric and do not linearise). Under the read's
pair-completion, a full $K$-\emph{cycle} telescopes to $\bigoplus (r_i \xor
r_{i+1}) = 0$ around the cycle --- the operand would go \textbf{bare}. Disjoint
pairs linearise to a nonzero $\bigoplus_i (r_{2i} \xor r_{2i+1})$, so the operand
is always masked. (Measured: cycle form $\to$ 6.3\% bare reads; disjoint pairs
$\to$ 0.13\%; with on-demand top-up $\to$ 0.000\%.)

\paragraph{Step 2 --- ordering.} Emit the identities with at most \doc{max\_open}
open per wire (a rolling window), interleaving the per-wire Open/Close event
streams randomly.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- control-wire updates (rerand), two kinds.} Splice band
updates $b \mathrel{\xor}= \mathrm{lit} \land \mathrm{lit}$ at sorted positions,
each kind's total split half data-control / half band-only:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\textsc{straddle}} (\doc{rerand\_level}): before the update,
  \emph{close} the open identities that read $b$ so none carries a stale $b$
  across it. Heavy straddle doses thin the masking (empirical knee $\approx
  1024$).
\item \textbf{\textsc{repair}} (\doc{rerand\_repair}): re-derive each straddling
  identity across the update --- emit its $b$-reading \g{} with old-$b$
  (removing its contribution), the update, then again with new-$b$ (re-adding
  it). Old-$b$ cancels, new-$b$ re-derives; the identity \emph{stays open}, so
  \textsc{repair} adds band turnover with \emph{no} thinning. Data controls draw
  only from live-data wires ($0..\mathit{active}$), which in the sandwich is the
  honest low half --- never the dead zero slice.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Step 4 --- embed $A$ with the masked (hidden-unmasking) read.}
Interleave $A$'s gates evenly through the scaffold. At each gate, for every
control:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{linearise} --- emit the reverse \g{} of every net-open mask so the
  wire carries $\mathit{operand} \xor \rho$, with $\rho$ a linear \textsc{xor}
  of band wires; top up on-demand with fresh complete linear pairs until the
  mask spans $\ge \doc{max\_open}$ pairs and $\rho$ is never empty (guarantees
  no bare read);
\item \textbf{fire} --- realise $c \mathrel{\xor}= \mathit{comp} \xor
  \mathrm{lit}(a) \land \mathrm{lit}(b)$ as the expansion $(a' \xor \rho_a)(b'
  \xor \rho_b)$ using only the masked control wires and band wires, never the
  bare $a$, $b$, or $a \xor b$ (0/1/2-control gates and all polarities handled;
  the 0-control fire is $\mathit{comp} \xor \textsc{and}(\emptyset) = \lnot
  \mathit{comp}$);
\item \textbf{de-linearise} --- undo (1) in reverse.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Step 5 --- no discipline pass.} The surrounding pipeline (junk-guard
zero-slice guards as modules 1 and 5) is unchanged.

\paragraph{Correctness} is verified exhaustively over all $2^n$ inputs $\times$
many band settings for $n \le 6$, $K \in 2..5$, all \doc{max\_open} and both
rerand kinds (\doc{scratchpad/v6}, 594 gadgets, 0 mismatches), and end-to-end in
\doc{gen\_sandwich\_gadget} (forward $+$ reverse-honesty sample-verify PASS).
$K \ge 2$ is required (a \g{} 1-cycle is a degenerate constant flip).

\section{Measurements}

\subsection{Global affine / degree-2 ridge (hiding of source-$C$ segments)}

Adversary: least-squares recovery of each source-$C$ wire-segment from a bounded
set of gadget wires (\doc{hmap\_affine -{}-degree 1/2}, ridge read with
\doc{reports/plot\_hmap\_ridge.py}). $\mathrm{meanH}$: $0 =$ recoverable,
$0.5 =$ fully hidden.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{meanH} \approx 0.498$ for every config $\Rightarrow$ $C$-segments
  hidden overall.
\item Affine ridge depth $\approx 0.055$, degree-2 $\approx 0.07$ (quadratic
  reaches $\sim0.02$ deeper into the low-degree I/O fringe). Both are
  \textbf{flat across $K = 2,4,8,16$} --- a wider per-\lgi{} mask buys no
  additional hiding, which is why the smallest $K$ wins.
\end{itemize}
\noindent (This sweeps $K$ = the mask \emph{width}; the identity's temporal
\emph{length} --- \S1(a) --- is held fixed circuit-spanning by the scaffold in
every config, so it is not the axis being varied here. The length claim of
\S1(a) rests on the HD/affine heatmap observation that short masks recede
locally and long ones do not, which is what motivated the circuit-spanning
scaffold.)

\subsection{Linearly-exposed source-$C$ segments --- the public-I/O fringe}

$\approx 8$--$10\%$ of source-$C$ segments are substantially linearly exposed
($\min$-$H < 0.25$ over cuts), flat across $K$ and $n$. They are \emph{not
scattered}: they are two smooth monotone ramps at the diagonal \textbf{endpoints}
--- the input side (segments affine in the \emph{known} input $x$, which the
adversary computes anyway) and the output side (converging on the delivered
$C(x)$). The interior \textbf{78\% of segments are fully hidden} ($H \approx
0.5$). This fringe is \textbf{unavoidable public I/O}, not a leak; the
boundary-cushion knobs (\doc{extra\_lgis}, \doc{a\_margin}) measured no effect
and are off by default. Fringe width $\approx 2 n_C$ gates = $C$'s
degree-growth depth.

\section{Tradeoffs and the current choices}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mask width $K$ vs size.} Larger $K$ widens each per-\lgi{} mask
  ($|\rho| \approx \doc{max\_open}\cdot K$) --- it does \emph{not} lengthen the
  identity's temporal reach, which the scaffold fixes circuit-spanning (\S1e).
  Empirically the hiding statistics (affine and degree-2 ridge, meanH,
  exposed-fringe fraction) are \emph{flat in $K$}: widening the local mask buys
  no additional hiding, while size grows $\sim$linearly and the masked-read cost
  grows \emph{quadratically} in $|\rho| \approx \doc{max\_open}\cdot K$. K16 (the
  historical choice) produces an $\sim$18M-gate gadget with no measured hiding
  gain over K2's $\sim$650k. \textbf{We choose K2}; K4 is available only when a
  wider read-mask ($\rho \approx 13$ vs $7$) is wanted. (The temporal-length
  lever of \S1(a) is not $K$ --- it is the scaffold spanning the whole circuit,
  on by construction.)
\item \textbf{Band turnover vs size (straddle vs repair).} More control-wire
  updates = more band re-randomisation and stronger resistance to
  commutation-back. But \textsc{straddle} updates \emph{thin} the masking past
  $\approx 1024$ (they close masks), degrading the ridge; \textsc{repair}
  updates preserve masking but \emph{grow} the gadget. We take \textbf{1000
  straddle} (at the safe knee) $+$ \textbf{3000 repair} (turnover without
  thinning), balancing band entropy against size.
\item \textbf{Read-mask width (\doc{max\_open}) vs read cost.} A wider $\rho$
  spreads each operand over more band wires locally, but the fire expands to
  $O(|\rho|^2)$ terms. $\doc{max\_open} = 3$ is the knee.
\item \textbf{The residual fringe is I/O, not a knob.} The $\sim$10\% exposed
  segments are the public input/output boundary; no parameter removes them (they
  are what the adversary already knows), so we do not pay size to chase them.
\end{itemize}
