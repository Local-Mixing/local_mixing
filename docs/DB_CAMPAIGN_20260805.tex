\begin{abstract}
This note reports a campaign of roughly seventy fmix runs that measured the
frozen-store DB move along four axes --- window \emph{geometry} (convex vs
contiguous), \emph{prefix descent}, \emph{window length} $s_{db}$, and
\emph{curation} --- in both COMP and MIX modes, on material ranging from
fresh gadget output to near-minimal circuits. Four results dominate.
(1)~Convex beats contiguous on every axis in COMP: $16\times$ the gates
removed, $7\times$ the ancestry transport, in $1/31$ the wall time; the
shipped default was inverted and has been fixed. (2)~Prefix descent is not a
size lever but a \emph{transport} lever, worth $600\times$ on ancestry while
being $6\times$ \emph{worse} on size per CPU-second. (3)~The MIX-DB move is a
mean-reversion operator on window length with a fixed point near~5: short
windows expand, long windows compress, and $s_{db}$ is therefore a
net-growth-rate knob rather than a search-width knob. (4)~The curated store
is a \emph{wide-window} store --- worthless at length~1 and worth $19\times$
at length~20. We close with the GSS profile, the settings for running fmix on
a gadgetized sliced sandwich, and with a section on the measurement traps
this campaign walked into, which we think is the most reusable part.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Design}

descent on/off $\times$ convex/contiguous $\times$ reshuffle on/off $\times$
curated on/off. 500k moves, seed 909, \code{--db-mode comp --p-comp 1.0
--p-any 0.0}, \code{--anc-samples 256}. All 16 convex arms and all 8
no-descent contiguous arms completed; the 8 descent$\times$contiguous arms are
$\sim$30$\times$ slower and are reported separately.

\subsection{Curated in COMP: the contract holds on size, fails on entropy}

Curated is worth $+1.0\%$ on size --- confirming the ``COMP is regular-only''
contract on that axis --- but it also uses \emph{2.4\% fewer} attempts (it
hits earlier in the cascade), gives $+8.1\%$ ancestry in all four matched
descent pairs, and multiplies selection entropy by $22\times$. Its extra hits
are size-neutral re-encodings, visible in gates/hit ($0.159$ with vs $0.187$
without). It supplies 38--41\% of successful splices under descent and
61--62\% without. \textbf{Recommendation: reverse the contract} --- the
original was written on the size axis alone.

\subsection{A bug: the shipped COMP sampler was inverted}

\code{--p-convex-comp} shipped at \textbf{0.1} from the 2026-08-03 defaults
commit, i.e.\ 90\% contiguous, when the intent was the reverse. Every run
taking the COMP default before 2026-08-04 spent $\sim$90\% of its COMP DB
budget on the geometry that loses on every axis above. Fixed to 0.9 in
\code{46350179}. MIX's \code{--p-convex 0.4} was left alone: no MIX run had
ever varied geometry, so there was no measurement behind it either way.

\section{Part II: per-length structure}

\subsection{Method}

With \code{--no-db-prefixes} the window length is drawn \emph{uniformly} from
$1..s_{db}$, so ``hit rate at length $k$'' is a genuine per-length conversion
rate. With descent on, the same counter would be conditional on every longer
length having already failed. Every table in this part uses the uniform draw,
$s_{db}=20$, 600k moves --- about 30k independent attempts per length.

Short windows almost always find a longer spelling and expand; long windows
almost always find a shorter one and compress. Over the run, lengths 1--4
added 556k gates and lengths 5--20 clawed back 250k, net $+313$k. So
\textbf{$s_{db}$ in MIX is a net-growth-rate knob}, not a search-width knob:
raising it dilutes expansion with self-compression. This is a second lever on
growth alongside \code{p\_mix}, which the layer-2 controller does not
currently know about.

Each geometry has its own fixed point: convex$+$curated $\approx5$,
convex$-$curated $\approx5.5$, contiguous$+$curated $\approx6.5$. That is why
contiguous grew \emph{more} (483{,}839 vs 412{,}797 final) despite adding about
the same at lengths 1--2: it compresses less above the crossover.

across all fourteen of them while removing 31{,}372; 98.6\% of all expansion
came from lengths 1--4 on 20\% of the draws, and 77\% from lengths 1--2 alone.

\section{Part III: MIX $s_{db}\times$ geometry at matched size}

\subsection{Design}

Arms are compared at \emph{matched final size}, not matched moves: MIX with
pay-random grows, and net growth per unit work falls monotonically with
$s_{db}$, so a fixed move budget would leave every arm at a different size and
a low-$s_{db}$ arm would ``win'' transport merely by being bigger. Each arm's
move budget was calibrated from a measured growth pass so it lands at
$\approx2\times$ the 100k input.

Convex at every $s_{db}$ (cheaper and more total entropy); low $s_{db}$ for
expansion efficiency (187 vs 1{,}509 moves per 1{,}000 gates grown).

But \textbf{est\_anc stays 1--10 in all twelve arms} --- pure MIX expansion to
$2\times$ transports essentially no ancestry at any setting. The braiding seen
in long p\_mix runs (est\_anc 12k--44k, carriers 0.98) comes from sustained
churn over millions of moves, not from the expansion leg. So this sweep
answers ``which $s_{db}$ expands best'' and \emph{not} ``which braids best at
held size''; the latter needs long runs with size pinned.

Under the phase-B reframe, where spread is judged in \emph{absolute} gates,
reach of $0.001$--$0.004$ on a 205k circuit is 200--820 positions, which is the
same order as the $\sim$850 that reframe calls meaningful. So these arms are
not as inert as the fractional numbers suggest.

\subsection{Evidence for each setting}

\subsection{Implementation}

Two mechanisms had to be added.

\emph{Geometry is now drawn once per round, before the length.} It used to be
drawn inside \code{sample\_window} \emph{after} the length was fixed, which
made a geometry-conditional length inexpressible and let the
best-of-\code{litter\_samples} selection compare windows drawn under different
geometries. New flags \code{--s-db-ctg} and \code{--s-db-comp-ctg}; resolution
is most-specific-first (mode$+$geometry $\rightarrow$ mode $\rightarrow$ base),
with 0 falling through rather than clamping.

\emph{Descent is now per-mode}, via \code{--db-prefixes-mix} and
\code{--db-prefixes-comp} (unset inherits the global \code{--db-prefixes}),
because the \code{--p-mix} overlay runs both modes in one process and they want
opposite settings.

Cleanup in the same change: \code{DbSample::Mixed} and \code{DbSample::parse}
were removed (nothing constructed or called them since the sampler knobs were
split per mode), and the DB banner now prints the \emph{effective} per-mode
settings rather than the base knobs.

\section{Part VI: measurement traps}

We think this is the most reusable section. Every one of these cost real runs.

\paragraph{Shipped defaults move under a rebuild.} \code{--db-prefixes} and
\code{--curated} both flipped ON between two builds of the same binary. A
launch script copied forward therefore silently changed the measurement. This
is fatal for per-length work: uniform draw gives a per-length conversion rate,
descent gives a quantity conditional on longer lengths failing. \emph{Always
pin every knob a measurement depends on.} Sanity check without reading the
banner: under a uniform draw a 600k-move run gives $\approx600\text{k}/s_{db}$
attempts per length; descent gives $\approx$600k at $s_{db}$ alone.

\paragraph{The COMP overrides shadow the base knobs.} \code{s\_db\_comp} (12)
and \code{p\_convex\_comp} (0.9) ship as concrete values, and
\code{active\_s\_db}/\code{active\_p\_convex} prefer them whenever the live mode
is COMP --- despite doc comments promising sentinel fall-through. A run passing
\code{--db-mode comp --s-db 20} silently gets 12. Detected because the COMP
per-length tables stopped at length 12 while the MIX ones reached 20.

\paragraph{\code{--target-size} does not cap DB-driven growth.} With
\code{--p-db 1.0} the DB move fires every round and the size brake does not
gate it. A MIX sweep intended to stop at 200k ran $s_{db}=3$ to 3{,}726{,}036
gates and OOM-killed six sibling jobs. Match sizes by \emph{budgeting moves}
from a measured growth calibration instead.

\paragraph{Hit rate is not a compression proxy.} \code{nR20\_mixed} has the
highest hit rates anywhere (47--77\% at lengths 3--6) and almost no removals;
\code{pre2\_100k} has the lowest (14--19\%) and removes far more. Hit rate
tracks \emph{g57-form fraction} (what the store can match); removal tracks
\emph{compressible fat}. Reading one for the other ranks these two circuits
exactly backwards.

\paragraph{Per-splice entropy inverts under aggregation.} Contiguous wins
bits/splice by $2.2\times$ and loses total entropy in all 8 matched pairs,
because it lands $2.4\times$ fewer splices. Always report both.

\paragraph{Budgeting by moves flatters whichever arm is slower per move.}
Descent ``wins'' size by 75\% at matched moves and loses by $6\times$ at matched
CPU. State the budget axis explicitly.

\section{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Matched-work no-descent} ($\approx$5M moves, $\approx$25 min).
The trajectories predict no-descent overtakes descent on size at equal CPU;
whether its ancestry percolates is unknown and would settle the COMP
configuration.
\item \textbf{$s_{db}$ 9/10 with descent in COMP.} The factorial is monotone
decreasing (12 beats 13) and 92\% of convex removals come from lengths $\le9$,
so the optimum may be lower than 12.
\item \textbf{MIX braiding at held size.} Part III measures expansion, not
transport. Long runs with size pinned would say which $s_{db}$ braids best.
\item \textbf{Layer 2 and $s_{db}$.} MIX $s_{db}$ is a second growth lever the
controller does not model.
\item \textbf{The remaining 2$\times$2$\times$2 cells.} MIX ctg $-$cur and COMP
ctg $-$cur were still running at the time of writing.
\end{enumerate}
