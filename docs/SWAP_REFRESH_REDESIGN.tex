\noindent
Redesign of the single-carrier production gadgetizer (the
\texttt{gen\_sandwich\_gadget} sandwich path): the aggregate-Gray fold is
replaced by a per-gate \emph{mask swap-with-refresh}, a \emph{closing
zero-slice block} is appended, and every measured output-port channel is
closed. The result eliminates all detected linear (and degree-2)
relations between the two circuits' wire segments \emph{and} is about
30\% smaller than the construction it replaces.

\section{Goal}

No $\mathrm{GF}(2)$-linear equation should hold in which some variables
are wire segments of the source (sandwich) circuit and others are wire
segments of the gadgetized circuit --- except through the public
boundary (functions of the shared input at the input port, of the shared
output at the output port), which any correct implementation exhibits.

The prior construction failed this in three measured ways.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Endpoint cancellation.} Every logical value decodes as
$v = c_v \XOR M_1 \XOR M_2 \XOR M_3 \XOR H \XOR \kappa$, the mask
monomials $M_i$ (degree 2) and $H$ (degree 3) being products of
\emph{frozen} band literals. A fold writes the control's decode onto the
target's carrier and never touches the target's own mask set, so the
masks cancel in every before/after XOR across the gate:
\[
\mathrm{carrier}^{+} \XOR \mathrm{carrier}^{-}
 \;=\; \mathrm{src}^{+} \XOR \mathrm{src}^{-},
\]
an exact cross-circuit linear identity --- measured by
\texttt{flip\_match} at 100\% of linear source gates and 32.4\% of
nonlinear ones.
\item \textbf{The Gray operand witness.} The aggregate-Gray fold gathers
an operand's complete mask sum onto an accumulator wire and strips it
back, so the operand is linearly reconstructible from three gadget
segments: $B = c_b \XOR u^{-} \XOR u^{+} \XOR \kappa$.
\item \textbf{Port asymmetry.} The zero-slice guard existed only at the
input port; a reverse evaluator met a structurally different entry.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Execution}

\subsection{Per-gate mask swap-with-refresh}

At every fold, the \emph{target} value and \emph{one randomly chosen
control} each retire one base-degree mask monomial and gain a freshly
drawn one. The target-side inject is emitted \emph{strictly interior} to
the fold's fragment stream; the control-side pair follows the fold,
pinned by read/write collisions. No contiguous window of any carrier's
writes then XORs to a clean operand decode.

Two lemmas fixed the mechanism's shape:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Verbatim moves are unsound.} Moving a monomial $M$ between
two mask sets emits it once on each touched wire, so over
$\mathrm{GF}(2)$ the two carriers' deltas XOR back to the source delta:
$\Delta(\text{t-carrier}) \XOR \Delta(\text{c-carrier}) = \Delta T$.
Conservation makes any exact move leak; arriving monomials must be
fresh draws.
\item \textbf{Fresh means fresh band positions.} A polarity re-roll of
the same product differs from it by a polynomial of degree
$\le \deg - 1$ in the band wires, whose values are themselves wire
segments --- back inside a linear adversary's span.
\end{itemize}

Swap mode \textbf{declines every Gray mode}: the gathers materialize an
operand's whole mask sum as an accumulator segment pair regardless of
mask ownership, and the gather/strip snapshot cannot tolerate a
mid-block registry change. The expanded fold is used instead, under a
\emph{target-stable commuting shuffle} that preserves per-wire write
order (same-target XOR writes commute; the standard shuffle re-exposed a
clean window at ${\sim}14\%$ of arity-1 folds).

\subsection{The closing zero-slice block}

An independently drawn slice guard with the opening block's
specification --- identity exactly on the zero band slice, every nonzero
band slice perturbs the data --- is appended after the mirror fill, so a
reverse evaluator meets the same structure at their entry as a forward
evaluator does. Its targets are confined to the low (forward-junk) data
half: the honest forward run reaches the output port with a junked band,
so the block fires there and must only perturb junk. The composite
preserves the sandwich on the \emph{upper data half only}, which is the
payload contract.

\subsection{Output-port hygiene}

Reaching zero required closing four further channels, each found by
re-running \texttt{flip\_match} after the previous fix:
the junk half is never stripped (bare junk segments hand their pair-XORs
to the source's own segment XORs); route-home runs \emph{before} the
strip, carrying the band-placement map, so routing swaps move masked
carriers; the strip's constant-discharge helper, both band fills, and
the epoch refills source exclusively from never-bared wires (a refill
copying a payload carrier into a band wire stores the payload bit where
a half-captured emission window can read it back); and every relocation
also refreshes one monomial of each moved value --- a payload value is a
fold target exactly once, so its mask function would otherwise recur
identically at every relocation stop, and a segment pair cutting through
two matching stops recovers the single value transition exactly.

\section{Why it ended up smaller}

The size reduction was not a goal; it fell out of declining Gray. The
Gray fold was never cheap --- it was bought for phase-A
store-reachability (narrow fragments; $31.55\% \to 95.47\%$) at a
measured $2.38\times$ the gates of the wide fold. Its per-gate machinery
--- gathering and stripping every mask atom of both operands onto two
accumulators, plus the four-phase product schedule --- costs more than
the expanded fold's fragment list at the production plan $[2,2,2,3]$.
The swap-refresh construction removes all of that and spends back only
four monomial emissions per fold, two per relocation, and one extra
slice block:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & prior (Gray) & swap-refresh & ratio \\
\midrule
32  & 80{,}950  & 57{,}217  & $0.71\times$ \\
64  & 157{,}586 & 112{,}394 & $0.71\times$ \\
128 & ${\sim}1.29$M (campaign builds) & 878{,}863 & ${\sim}0.68\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The $n{=}128$ half-sandwich build ($|C|{=}|D|{=}3000$) comes
to 481{,}070 gates.

The price is paid in a different currency: with the ladder off,
${\sim}7\%$ of fold fragments remain wide, material phase~A's store
cannot re-encode. Both measured remedies currently violate the
zero-relation contract (\texttt{--prod-ladder-cap 4} re-opens a small
linear tail through its borrowed scratch; \texttt{PROD\_POST\_FRAGMENT}
a broad one) and are off-limits for deliverables until audited. Store
reachability under the expanded fold is the open measurement for the
next mixing campaign.

\section{Verification ($n=128$, production parameters, fresh random seed)}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Function}: bit-sliced verify passed (payload half equals
the sandwich on the honest slice); 317/317 unit tests.
\item \textbf{Inputs$\leftrightarrow$outputs}: the rank of the 257
columns $\{1, x, y\}$ over 1024 samples is full ($257/257$) --- no
affine relation between inputs and outputs (conclusive: a true relation
forces a rank deficit on every sample set).
\item \textbf{\texttt{flip\_match}} (local segment-pair XOR vs per-gate
source deltas, kwin 12/30/60): $0/13{,}568$ source gates matched ---
down from 100\% linear / 32.4\% nonlinear on the prior build.
\item \textbf{\texttt{segment\_deduce}, degree 1} (8 cuts, 16{,}384
samples, fresh-sample verified): 2.91\% of sandwich segments deducible,
every equation on the shared-input or shared-output cut; \emph{zero}
interior-cut equations (prior build: ${\sim}50\%$ deducible with
thousands of interior equations in the gadget-exposure direction).
\item \textbf{\texttt{segment\_deduce}, degree 2} (four restricted
windows up to 12{,}881 monomials, both directions, matched degree-1
controls): degree 2 adds exactly one equation --- an early C-gate output
that is a quadratic of the \emph{public input}, matched on the input cut
only. No other verified equation uses a product; the reverse direction's
interior-labeled hits are the public payload $C(x)$ at the sandwich's
C/D seam matching the gadget's output, present for any correct
implementation. Caveats: windows are restricted, and products are
one-sided (no bilinear $s \cdot g$ cross-terms in the model).
\end{itemize}

\section{Operation}

\texttt{ProdConfig::production\_single()} now sets
\texttt{swap\_refresh: 1, close\_slice: 1}; environment overrides
\texttt{PROD\_SWAP} / \texttt{PROD\_CLOSE\_SLICE} (0/0 restores the
prior Gray stream byte-for-byte). The gss\_mix pipeline picks the new
defaults up unchanged. The two-share (\texttt{sss}) and multi-carrier
paths receive the fold-level swap wherever \texttt{fold\_cg} runs but
not the output-port hygiene; extending them is future work.
