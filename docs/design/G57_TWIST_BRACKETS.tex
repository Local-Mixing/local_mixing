\begin{abstract}
The \code{fmix} conjugation twist used to bracket its window with two 3-CNOT
swap packets --- six \code{comp=0} single-control gates per twist, a distinctive
syntactic fingerprint that the frozen store cannot digest. This note describes
the replacement (\code{--twist-g57}, commits \code{4e9dbf40} and
\code{83ae0d11}): each bracket is spelled as a shortest \emph{all-g57 word}
solved online per seam, sited adaptively so it absorbs neighborhood gates ---
the hidden-SAMF mechanism of \code{ssg}, made XGate-native. We describe the
word engine, the two-generation placer, and the measurement campaign:
A/B/C-schedule mixing runs at two twist rates against no-twist and legacy-twist
baselines, a placement factorial, an ancestry-instrument audit, and a
functional-legibility audit (degree-1/degree-2 affine plates and the stress
battery). Headline: at rate $0.002$ the new brackets give $\sim$40\% smaller
circuits and 5--7$\times$ the ancestry transport of the legacy packets; and
across \emph{all} twist variants and rates, no functional instrument in the
battery detects either harm or benefit from twisting at these doses --- the
one measurable twist effect is syntactic (write-census smearing).
\end{abstract}

\section{The bracket problem}

A twist rewrites $W \mapsto P\,(P W P)\,P \equiv W$: it conjugates a window's
interior in place and pays for two bracket packets $P$. With
$P = \mathrm{swap}(a,b)$ realized as 3 CNOTs per side, every twist deposits six
\code{comp=0} single-control gates. Three problems: (i) the packets are a
syntactic fingerprint (nothing else in the mixed bulk looks like them);
(ii) the frozen store's match rate collapses on CNOT-bearing windows (the
$m{=}6$ cliff), so the packets are indigestible and accumulate; (iii) they are
pure cost --- placed uniformly at random, nothing ever cancels.

The fix follows the \code{ssg} hidden-SAMF idea: spell the bracket in the bulk
material's own alphabet (g57 gates, \code{comp=1} width-2), and \emph{site} it
where the neighborhood pays part of the bill.

\section{The swap-word engine}

The bracket seam only needs the shortest all-g57 word for a
\emph{permutation}: $R \equiv \mathit{ctx}\cdot S$ at the opening seam,
$S\cdot \mathit{ctx}$ at the closing seam, where $\mathit{ctx}$ is a few real
neighborhood gates and $S = \mathrm{swap}(a,b)$. This is answerable online,
with no precomputed replacement database: on 4 abstract wires a 16-state
permutation packs into one \code{u64}; the radius-4 BFS ball over the 24 g57
gates (165{,}443 permutations) builds once in $\sim$10\,ms, and a
meet-in-the-middle scan answers any target up to word length 7 in
$\sim$200--350\,$\mu$s. The tables are target-independent: one engine serves
every context shape, any consumption depth, 3-wire supports, non-g57
neighbors, and (later) the negation twist variants. An earlier design ---
a SQLite enumeration of 218k syntactic contexts requiring offline synthesis
--- was retired in favor of this after its $\le k{+}3$ replacement contract
proved unachievable at $k{=}2$ in its own scope (every such context sits at
distance 6).

Ground facts (exhaustive; Appendix~A for the full tables):
$\mathrm{dist}(S) = 6$ over the g57 alphabet on 3 \emph{and} 4 wires ---
the fourth wire buys nothing; there are exactly \textbf{64} minimal words
(32 per choice of helper wire, none genuinely 4-wire). Consuming $k$
neighborhood gates nets $6-2k$ when they cancel: $k{=}1$ always possible in
the abstract (8 of 24 gates are attachable for fixed $S$), $k{=}2$ net $+2$,
$k{=}3$ net $0$ (48\% of same-support triples), and with \emph{non-g57}
context (worth several g57s each) a seam can net \emph{negative} --- a twist
that shrinks the circuit.

\section{The placer}

\textbf{v1 (anchor-first).} Candidate wire pairs $(a,b)$ are seeded from the
boundary gates' own pins (a uniformly random $b$ almost never lands inside
the 4-wire seam support), plus one uniform pair to keep fresh-wire routing on
the menu. Both seams are solved for every candidate; cheapest total net wins.
Each seam may consume up to 3 context gates of any shape. Every splice is
verified against the reference 3-CNOT packet under local verification.

\textbf{v1's failure mode.} The per-seam net histogram showed almost exactly
\emph{one} bare (+6) seam per twist: the closing bracket inherits $(a,b)$
from the opening seam, and its fixed neighbor almost never pins both wires.

\textbf{v2 (slide + joint acceptance).} Two composable fixes, each with an
env kill-switch for A/Bs (\code{TWIST\_G57\_NO\_SLIDE},
\code{TWIST\_G57\_NO\_RETRY}; both default on):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Slide}: a seam that stays bare walks its bracket outward (up to
512 gates, extending the conjugated window over what it passes --- window
length was a random draw, and a relabel is far cheaper than the word the
slide saves) to the next g57 pinning both twist wires.
\item \emph{Joint acceptance}: both ends are solved before committing; a
window whose best plan nets worse than $+8$ in total is redrawn (up to 4
tries), so one side stays bare only when its partner's match pays for it
(net $\le +2$) or every redraw failed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Always-advance.} Every inserted gate takes the ballistic
birth-advance unconditionally (the \code{--db-advance} treatment, aimed
outward per bracket) --- bracket material rides away from the seam instead of
sitting on the window edge. This is independent of the \code{--db-advance}
flag itself, which still governs DB splice products only.

\textbf{Ancestry.} Consumed context unions its litters' ancestor sets into
the replacement litter, exactly as a DB splice would (v1 dropped them ---
an instrument bug worth $+1.5$--$10\%$ of \code{anc}, isolated by rerunning
v1-placement arms with only the recording changed).

At matched \emph{effective work} (cumulative moves per gate), the ancestry cost
of twisting is: legacy $-89\%$ vs.\ no-twist, g57 $-45\%$ --- the new
brackets keep 5--6$\times$ more transport at equal dose, \emph{and} end
smaller, despite emitting more gates per twist ($\sim$8--9 net vs.\ 6): the
brackets are store-digestible, so COMP chews them back down instead of
accumulating CNOT bulk.

\textbf{Placement factorial} (C arm, rate 0.01): mean net gates per seam
$4.51$ (v1) $\to 4.38$ (slide only) $\to 3.93$ (retry only) $\to
\mathbf{3.72}$ (both); bare seams $50\%\to35\%$; size $216\mathrm{k}\to
168\mathrm{k}$; anc $637\to921$. The joint-acceptance retry is the larger
lever; the slide composes. Cost: $2.6\times$ the MITM solves and $\sim$1.6
extra window draws per twist --- microseconds per move at production rates.

\section{Is the ancestry drop a mixing loss? The instrument audit}

Twists still cost \code{anc}/\code{span} relative to no-twist even under v2.
Two audits asked whether that reading is real.

\textbf{(1) Recording audit.} A conditional census over all run states
(parsing litters and ancestor sets) shows the reported \code{anc} was
\emph{already} conditional on non-empty sets, and the empty-ancestry gate
fraction is moderate (3--21\% for g57, up to 46\% for legacy at 0.01) --- so
the drop is not mean-deflation by ancestry-less brackets; conditioning
changes nothing about the ordering. The union-recording fix (above) moves
\code{anc} by only $+1.5$--$10\%$.

\textbf{(2) Functional audit.} Affine-reconstruction plates
(\code{hmap\_affine}) of the \emph{input circuit's} prefix progress against
each mixed circuit, read by the ridge measure:
\begin{itemize}
\item Degree 1: identical for all seven arms ($\rho = 1.000$, perm
$z \approx 7.1$, depth $0.22$--$0.26$). \emph{Caveat discovered in the
process:} every current twist type is an affine conjugation and the degree-1
measure is affine-invariant --- it is \textbf{blind to twist effects by
construction}, in both directions.
\item Degree 2 (2{,}081 regressors --- not affine-blind): the diagonal is at
ceiling in every arm ($\rho = 1.000$, $z \approx 4.5$); depth $0.305$
(no twist) vs.\ $0.26$--$0.30$ (twisted) --- at most a marginal hint of
benefit, within noise.
\item Stress battery (bias-sensitive statistical probes; single-wire /
XOR-pair predictors on state bits, parities, and trajectory bits): no
benefit --- prominence $0.26$--$0.31$ above floor in \emph{every} arm
including no-twist, with progress monotonicity $\rho = 0.85$--$0.99$,
$z = 7$--$9$ everywhere. The progress clock is readable by near-trivial
predictors in all arms; no twist dose or variant dents it.
\item The one measurable twist effect is \emph{syntactic}: the per-wire
write-count bimodality gap falls from $0.283$ (no twist) to $0.09$--$0.22$
under twists.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Verdict.} At these doses, twisting costs real ancestry transport and
size while buying nothing measurable on functional channels; its measurable
value is syntactic-census smearing. The ancestry gap \emph{between twist
variants} is therefore not a mixing-quality gap --- but the case for twisting
at all now rests on adversary classes not yet in the battery. Practical
recommendation: keep \code{--p-twist} at $0.002$ or below, use
\code{--twist-g57} so the dose costs 5--6$\times$ less, and treat any
proposal to raise twist rates as requiring a demonstrated adversary it
defeats.

\section{Usage}

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{--p-twist R --twist-g57} --- the full v2 path: all-g57 words,
anchor-first seams, context consumption, slide, joint acceptance,
unconditional birth-advance, ancestry union. Pure swap only
(\code{--twist-neg-p} is ignored on this path).
\item \code{TWIST\_G57\_NO\_SLIDE=1} / \code{TWIST\_G57\_NO\_RETRY=1} ---
env kill-switches for the two v2 placement features (A/B use).
\item Omit \code{--twist-g57} for the legacy 3-CNOT swap-family packets
(\code{--twist-neg-p} active there).
\item \code{--db-advance} --- separate flag; birth-advance for \emph{DB
splice} products. The g57 bracket path advances its insertions regardless.
\item Report line: \code{twist-g57: consumed= emitted= net/seam[hist]
solves= avg\_us= slides= retries=}. Note: this path emits
\code{ORIGIN\_SYNTH} \code{comp=1} material, so \code{comp=}/\code{shaped=}
read population form under it (same caveat as \code{p\_db>0}).
\item Tuning constants (compile-time, \code{mix.rs}):
\code{TG\_SLIDE\_CAP}=512, \code{TG\_SLIDE\_TRIES}=3, \code{TG\_RETRIES}=4,
\code{TG\_ACCEPT\_NET}=+8.
\end{itemize}

\section{The replacement table}

The complete machine-generated table lives in
\code{docs/G57\_TWIST\_REPLACEMENTS.txt}: \textbf{T1}, all 64 minimal
(length-6) all-g57 words realizing $S=\mathrm{swap}(a,b)$ (32 per helper
wire; gate $[t,n,p]$ means $t \mathrel{\hat{}}= n \lor \lnot p$);
\textbf{T2}, the 12 ordered pair-consumption identities on $\{a,b,c\}$
($h_1\,h_2\,S \equiv R$ with $|R|=4$, net $+2$); \textbf{T3}, the 8
single-gate attachment identities ($h\,S \equiv R$ with $|R|=5$, net $+4$).
The engine does not read this table --- it re-derives everything from the
BFS ball at startup --- but the table is the exhaustive human-checkable
inventory of what the seams can do at $k\le2$ on a 3-wire support.
