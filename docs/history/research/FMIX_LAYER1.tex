\begin{abstract}
\noindent This describes \texttt{fmix}'s per-round move menu as it now stands,
after the restructure of 27--28 July. It is a reference for what the system
does, not a design history; where a measurement has since argued against a
design choice, the measurement is stated inline. The phase overlay --- the rule
engine that changes parameters mid-run --- is layer 2 and does not exist yet.
Design rationale is in \texttt{docs/FMIX_MENU.md}; the ancestry measurements are
in \texttt{docs/ANCESTRY_INSTRUMENTATION.pdf}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The round}

Every move is one round. Four slots, evaluated in order; the first that fires
consumes the round.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
\# & slot & fires with & effect on size \\
\midrule
0 & conditions & always & none \\
1 & twist & $p_{\text{twist}}$ & $+2$ / $+6$ before absorption \\
2 & DB move & $p_{\text{db}}$ & depends on \texttt{db_mode} \\
3 & thermostat & otherwise & $\pm$small \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Slot 3 runs \emph{iff the slot-2 coin did not fire}. A slot-2 round whose
descent finds nothing is spent --- there is no fallthrough --- so the thermostat
receives exactly $(1-p_{\text{twist}})(1-p_{\text{db}})$ of rounds regardless of
how hard the material is.

\paragraph{Slot 0.} Two hardcoded conditions: the pool rebuild on its cadence,
and the size brake (\S\ref{sec:brake}). The stop checks --- flag, canary, dose
--- run at the report point. A general condition$\to$action rule engine is layer 2.

\section{The DB move}

One operation with three admission rules. The rule is selected by
\texttt{db_mode} at slot 2, and fixed by role on the thermostat branches.

\subsection{Window selection and the descent}

Draw a seed gate (\S\ref{sec:pool}); sample $s_{db}$ gates around it, convex with
probability $p_{\text{convex}}$ else contiguous. Both gather until the block is
contiguous in the arena. A gate with $\ge w_{\text{window}}$ controls is
ineligible; a window containing one is truncated before it.

The descent then tries the window and, on failure, drops one gate and retries,
down to length 1. \textbf{The gate dropped is the one farthest from the seed},
so the seed survives to the shortest rung --- dropping from a fixed end instead
walks away from the very gate the descent exists to re-encode, on roughly half
of contiguous windows and \emph{every} convex one.

\paragraph{The bottom rung always pays.} A single gate's permutation has exactly
one one-gate implementation: itself. So at length 1 the free branch is
unreachable and MIX-DB either finds nothing or pays the smallest non-identical
spelling. Every descent therefore ends in a paid expansion or a genuine miss,
which is what makes per-gate miss accounting unnecessary. Measured: the length-1
rung is the busiest of all, carrying 24\% of splices.

\subsection{The identity guard}

A candidate identical to the outgoing window, gate for gate, is never spliced,
in all three modes. Splicing one is a no-op that still costs a round and still
stamps a generation --- a re-encoding the dose meter would count and that did not
happen. Measured: it refuses roughly \textbf{one candidate in six}.

\subsection{The three modes}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
mode & admission & selection \\
\midrule
ANY-DB & any size & uniform over all non-identical equivalents \\
MIX-DB & free if possible, else pay & uniform over non-identical $\le$ window; \\
       &                            & else uniform over the smallest \\
COMP-DB & only if some match $\le$ window & uniform over the minimum-size ones \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The asymmetry between MIX and COMP is deliberate: COMP is a contraction move so
minimum size is its job, while MIX is a re-encoding move so entropy is. MIX-DB
makes the ingest-versus-pay decision \emph{per window}, from the match list the
lookup already returned --- which is what replaced an entire per-gate tier state
machine.

\paragraph{Where each is used.} Slot 2 uses \texttt{db_mode}, a deterministic
three-way flag. The contract branch is always COMP-DB (it must not grow); the
expand branch is always ANY-DB (it exists to grow).

\paragraph{Selection is lazy.} Every rule is a function of gate counts, and the
stored value is a flat length-prefixed sequence, so candidates are catalogued by
walking offsets and \emph{only the winner is decoded}. A candidate that fails to
place, or turns out to be the window, is dropped and the choice retried.

\subsection{The curated store}

Optional (\texttt{--curated}), off by default. Curated entries are halves of
split minimal identities, so their internal pieces are not locally compressible
--- a route \texttt{fcompress} cannot partially undo. Curated-ness is a
lexicographic first key: a non-identical curated match beats a regular one
regardless of size, with the mode's own rule applied inside the winning class.
COMP is exempt.

\paragraph{Forward key only.} Probing curated on the reverse canonical key
returns entries belonging to a \emph{different} permutation. A store-level probe
of one window found 430{,}568 curated candidates, all from the reverse key,
\emph{none} equivalent; the forward key returned none, and the regular store
returned 6, all equivalent. A curated replacement that still fails verification
is refused and counted rather than fatal, and \texttt{--curated} refuses
\texttt{--no-db-verify}.

\paragraph{Status: not runnable.} The store's fan-in per key is enormous, and
even with lazy decoding the catalogue walk is linear in it. Curated runs are
roughly $30\times$ slower than uncurated and have not completed.

\section{Twists}

Slot 1 is the only twist channel. Type is drawn from the three
\texttt{w_twist_*} weights as ratios; window length is log-uniform over
$[\texttt{twist_min_len}, |C|]$ with the virtual start drawn symmetrically, so
head and tail accumulate coverage equally.

\paragraph{Placement.} A bracket dropped at random must be paid for; one dropped
next to a gate that can swallow it costs nothing. \texttt{TWIST_PATTERNS} is a
table of welcoming shapes; the placer samples up to
\texttt{twist_place_tries} candidate positions and falls back to the random draw
otherwise, reporting \texttt{tplace=placed/fallback} so an inert table is
visible. One entry so far: a \texttt{comp=1} gate absorbs a NOT on its target and
comes out \texttt{comp=0}, eroding a fossil in the bargain. That absorption
needed a \texttt{merge_result} extension --- it is always mathematically legal,
but the catalogue previously refused it at width $\ge 2$.

\paragraph{Recorded, unconsumed.} A verified hidden-swap identity, in g57
notation $[x,y,z] = x \mathbin{\hat{=}} y \vee \neg z$:
\[
  [a,b,c]\cdot \mathrm{swap}(a,b)\cdot [b,c,a] \;=\; [b,a,c]\cdot[b,c,a]\cdot[a,b,c]\cdot[a,c,b]
\]
checked exhaustively on all 8 inputs. Two g57s bracketing a swap equal four g57s
and \emph{no swap}, so a swap conjugation sited between a matching pair costs
$+2$ gates rather than $+6$ and leaves no swap-shaped fingerprint. Consuming it
needs a rewrite path, which is a different operation from siting a bracket.

\paragraph{Cost.} Measured at 500k moves: the interior relabel costs
\textbf{0.4\%} of runtime at $p_{\text{twist}}=0.002$, and 28\,ns per
gate-visit. A deferred-relabel ledger was designed and then dropped on that
evidence.

\section{The thermostat}

\[
  p(\text{contract}) = \sigma\!\left(\frac{\text{size}-\texttt{target_size}}{\texttt{temp}}\right),
  \quad \text{clamped to } [0.02,\ \texttt{contract_ceiling}]
\]

\texttt{target_size} sets where the equilibrium sits; \texttt{temp} is the width
of the transition band, not a rate --- small \texttt{temp} gives tight regulation
and fast approach, large gives breathing.

\textbf{Contract} tries COMP-DB with probability $p_{\text{comp}}$, then journal
undo, then the merge catalogue. \textbf{Expand} is an ANY-DB move with
probability $p_{\text{any}}$, else a cross. Unsubsume, insert and fresh-split are
retired.

Two consequences worth stating. The contraction ceiling stays at $0.98$: its 2\%
expansion floor is a structural growth source, but it is also what keeps
crossings --- hence fossil erosion --- running at target, and tightening it to
$0.9995$ starves erosion by $40\times$. And with the catalogue-invertible
expansions gone, \textbf{deep contraction is COMP-DB's job}: crossing ladders are
not pairwise-recoverable, so without a store the drain has only the journal.

\paragraph{Caveat.} At phase-A rates ($p_{\text{db}}$ near 1) slot 3 barely runs,
so \texttt{target_size} and \texttt{temp} set the contract/expand mix rather than
the size. Size control there comes from the brake.

\section{Seeding and the generation pool}\label{sec:pool}

Every gate carries a generation: inputs start at 0, a splice stamps the outgoing
window's upper-median $+1$, splits stamp parent $+1$, merges take the min, twist
brackets are born-random.

Seed selection is one coin: with probability $p_{\text{mingen}}$ draw from the
\emph{generation pool}, else uniformly. The pool is the
$\texttt{pool_k}$ lowest-generation gates that are \textbf{pool-eligible}
($< w_{\text{pool}}$ controls) \textbf{and below the goal}. Both filters carry
weight: an ineligible gate can never be re-encoded, so an unfiltered pool
converges on exactly that set; and without the below-goal filter a late pool
fills with ordinary finished gates and the canary can never fire.

$\texttt{pool_k}$ is a \emph{count}, not a fraction, because the drain rate is
set by the move economy and is independent of circuit size.

\paragraph{Two eligibility thresholds.} $w_{\text{window}}$ governs what may sit
\emph{inside} a window; $w_{\text{pool}}$ what may \emph{seed} one and count
toward the dose. They differ because width-3 gates match in context often enough
to admit to windows but re-encode end-to-end at 0.41\% against 98.98\% for width
$\le 2$, so at a shared threshold they accumulate in the pool with nothing to
eject them.

\section{The canary}

Over a ring buffer of the last \texttt{canary_window} \emph{qualifying} rounds,
the fraction that failed at every rung. A round qualifies only if its seed
genuinely came from the pool; a heads coin that fell through a drained pool is
counted separately, because that means the rebuild is too slow rather than that
the material is unreachable --- opposite remedies. The condition fires when the
buffer is full and the fraction exceeds $\theta$, and it stops the run
(\texttt{MixStop::CanaryFired}), checked ahead of the dose stop.

It \textbf{sleeps while the brake holds COMP}: COMP declines far more often by
construction, and since this is a stop condition those samples would end runs for
a reason unrelated to reachability.

\section{Size control and breathing}\label{sec:brake}

Growth past \texttt{size_hi} forces slot 2 into COMP; it is released at
\texttt{size_lo} \emph{or} when COMP's shed rate over a trailing window falls
below \texttt{comp_release_eps}, whichever comes first.

The productivity release is what makes a \emph{wide} band safe, and a wide band
is what transport wants, since growth legs are where material moves. The danger
was never width but sitting in COMP past its usefulness, where it starves as the
circuit nears local minimality and spends re-encoding diversity --- COMP draws
only from minimum-size spellings, pulling toward exactly the form
\texttt{fcompress} would compute anyway.

\paragraph{Measured, manually.} A 2M-move exhale at $s_{db}=12$ shed
$-0.060$ gates/move against $-0.054$ at $s_{db}=9$ --- \textbf{COMP wants a
longer window than MIX}. Mixing did \emph{not} degrade: \texttt{anc},
\texttt{span} and litter diversity all rose. The only cost was \texttt{dmin},
$0.168 \to 0.136$. No bend in the shed rate appeared in 2M moves.

\section{Litter rules}

Every gate carries a litter id --- the replacement event that created it --- and
its litter's birth size. Splits propagate the id, a splice mints a fresh one, a
merge unions. Two optional rules, both off by default and neither yet A/B'd:
\texttt{--litter-ban} refuses a rung that is exactly one complete litter (the
unit an earlier replacement emitted, and so where the store can hand that
spelling straight back), and \texttt{--litter-samples} draws several candidate
windows and keeps the most litter-diverse.

Measured: at equilibrium roughly 70 full-litter splices per 100k moves, and
windows drawing from only $\sim$3 distinct litters against a ceiling of $s_{db}$
--- reached almost immediately, not a transient. That gap is the largest visible
untouched lever.

\section{Instrumentation}

Beyond the long-standing report line:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
field & meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{idsk} & candidates refused by the identity guard \\
\texttt{cur} & curated hits / curated rejections \\
\texttt{g57only} & g57-only COMP attempts and hits (prints hits / rounds) \\
\texttt{dmin} & fraction of windows admitting a strictly shorter spelling \\
\texttt{osyn} & fraction of gates whose ancestry label is destroyed \\
\texttt{anc}, \texttt{ancspan} & mean ancestor-set size and span \\
\texttt{distinct}, \texttt{full}, \texttt{ban} & litter diversity, complete-litter splices, bans \\
\texttt{tplace} & twists placed on a pattern / fallen back \\
\texttt{canary}, \texttt{cft} & canary failure fraction, pool fall-throughs \\
\texttt{g57} & true g57 census (comp=1, two opposite-polarity controls) \\
\texttt{shaped} & the same census with polarity ignored --- see below \\
\texttt{polf} & $(\texttt{shaped}-\texttt{g57})/\texttt{shaped}$: the twist odometer \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Reading \texttt{shaped} against \texttt{g57}.} These two answer
different questions and the single \texttt{g57} field used to conflate them.
The store emits g57 circuits and nothing else --- measured directly, with
twists disabled \texttt{g57/comp} holds at $1.000$ for a whole run --- so
\texttt{shaped} is the DB-effectiveness reading: $1-\texttt{shaped}/\text{size}$
is exactly the material the DB did not produce.

\texttt{polf} is a twist dose, not erosion. A negation twist conjugates its
window by NOT on one wire, flipping the polarity of every literal on it. A
g57's two controls lie on distinct wires, so a twist touches at most one, and
touching one flips the pair from opposite to same: the gate keeps its
\texttt{comp}, its width and its place, and only stops matching the strict
shape. Swap twists carry polarity with the wire and leave \texttt{polf} at
zero. A/B at equal twist count over 400k moves gives \texttt{g57/comp} =
$1.000$ (no twists), $0.704$ (neg), $1.000$ (swap, at $2.6\times$ the relabel
count), while \texttt{comp}/size never leaves $0.996$.

Unlike \texttt{cov}, whose denominator is the growing circuit, \texttt{polf} is
bounded in $[0,\tfrac12]$ and cannot fall while twists keep firing.

Plus two extra lines: the \textbf{splice size histogram} (joint outgoing
$\to$ incoming length, which the scalar totals cannot show) and, under
\texttt{--ancestors}, the \textbf{ancestry report} with log-bucketed histograms
of \texttt{anc}, span and fan-out.

\paragraph{Do not quote \texttt{odiff}, \texttt{oadj} or \texttt{disp} without
\texttt{osyn}.} All three skip gates whose ancestry label a mixed-lineage splice
destroyed, so they are computed over the material mixing has failed to touch and
grow more selective the better it works. \texttt{osyn} reaches 50.7\% by 1M moves
and 1.000 on small circuits, where those three are undefined rather than merely
degraded.

\section{Checkpoint and resume}

\texttt{--state-out} writes a resume file at every stop, and one is written
beside each \texttt{--snap-every-moves} snapshot. \texttt{--resume} rebuilds the
chain; \texttt{--input} is then not required.

The circuit file alone cannot do this. Per-gate direction has no sidecar and the
whole directional walk rides on it; the undo journal references arena ids, so the
checkpoint renumbers to arena order and remaps, dropping entries with dead
pieces; and the \emph{original} circuit is what \texttt{global_check} verifies
against, so a resumed run without it would verify nothing about fidelity to the
true input. Also carried: moves, event and litter counters, the tabu queue,
\texttt{twspan}, the canary ring, \texttt{db_mode_cur} and brake state, the pool,
ancestor sets, and every counter. \texttt{StdRng} is not serialisable, so a fresh
seed is drawn and stored --- a clean continuation, not a bit-identical replay.
The file is version-stamped and refuses to load across versions.

\textbf{A resume takes every parameter from the command line}, including
\texttt{--db-mode} and \texttt{--p-mingen}. The saved mode is diagnostic only.

\section{What is not built}

\begin{itemize}
\item The slot-0 rule engine. The brake and canary are hardcoded.
\item \texttt{twist_db_mode} and DB absorption of CNOT packets; the hidden-swap
identity is verified but unconsumed, and wants table entries to be rewrite rules
rather than site hints.
\item A bounded candidate walk for curated, without which it cannot run.
\item Store guards read from store metadata rather than retyped per run.
\item A forced pool rebuild on COMP $\to$ MIX.
\end{itemize}
