\section{The problem: the progress diagonal}

The gadgetizer rewrites a small circuit $C$ into a much larger equivalent $G$
whose gates operate on \textbf{secret-shared} logical values. The security goal
is that $G$ leak nothing about $C$ beyond its input/output behaviour---in
particular, that an adversary cannot tell \emph{where in the original
computation} any point of $G$ sits.

The standard sharing is a linear XOR pair: a logical value $v$ is carried on two
wires $c_0,c_1$ with $v=c_0\xor c_1$. This is the root of a persistent leak.
Because the decode is linear, $v$ is a \textbf{degree-1 (affine) function of the
wires at every snapshot}. The \texttt{hmap\_affine} tool measures exactly this:
for each prefix $i$ of $C$ and prefix $j$ of $G$, it fits the best $\mathrm{GF}(2)$
affine reconstruction of $C$'s state-$i$ from $G$'s wires at $j$ and reports the
holdout error $H$ ($0$ = perfectly recoverable, $0.5$ = hidden). With linear
sharing, at the aligned prefix $C_i$ is recoverable from the shares regardless of
$C_i$'s algebraic complexity in the input, so a low-$H$ \textbf{diagonal} runs
down the map. That diagonal reveals the temporal layout of the computation, and
it \textbf{survives every mixing method} tried (ssg, fmix, generation
targeting): mixing re-randomises the sharing, but the value stays affine in the
new wires, so the diagonal persists at $\rho=1$.

The measure is read by the \textbf{ridge}, never the mean (which saturates near
$0.5$): \emph{depth} = mean per-row prominence of the low-$H$ valley above its
row background; $\rho$ = ``is it a monotone diagonal''; \emph{perm-z} =
significance vs.\ a shuffled null.

\section{The encoding}

Replace the pair decode with a \textbf{balanced nonlinear} one. Each logical
value carries, on top of its XOR pair, $k$ permanent product mask terms sourced
on a frozen band of extra wires:
\[
v_i \;=\; \underbrace{w_{u(i)}\xor w_{u'(i)}}_{\text{linear carrier}}
\;\xor\; \underbrace{\bigoplus_{j}\ \prod_{l}\bigl(w_{p_{jl}}\xor a_{jl}\bigr)}_{k\text{ product terms}}
\;\xor\; \underbrace{c_i}_{\text{ledger const}} .
\]
Each product term is a conjunction of $\deg$ band wires with per-factor offsets
$a_l$; $c_i$ is a ledger constant. The offsets and $c$ are compile-time bits
kept in the gadgetizer's ledger.

\section{Why the linear carrier is not optional---the balance obstruction}

One might hope to drop the XOR pair and carry $v$ as a \textbf{pure} two-wire
product $v=(w_p\xor a)(w_q\xor b)$. This is impossible to gate.

\textbf{Claim.} No exact reversible gadget can conditionally flip a value whose
decode has unequal class sizes---with any ancillae, garbage, or re-encoding.

\emph{Argument.} A pure 2-wire product decode is unbalanced: over the four
$(w_p,w_q)$ states, $v=1$ has exactly one preimage $(\neg a,\neg b)$ and $v=0$
has three (a \textbf{3:1} imbalance). Consider realising any gate that must
conditionally flip a value---e.g.\ $\mathrm{CNOT}\colon v_t \mathrel{\xor}= v_x$.
Exactness forces the gadget $F$ (a reversible map on all wires, ancillae
included, with any new ledger afterward) to biject the state-class
$\{v_x=\alpha,\,v_t=\beta\}$ onto $\{v_x=\alpha,\,v_t=\beta\xor\alpha\}$.
Counting representations and cancelling common factors, the $\alpha=0$ equations
give $R'(0)/R'(1)=R(0)/R(1)$ and the $\alpha=1$ equations give
$R'(0)/R'(1)=R(1)/R(0)$, where $R,R'$ are the target's rep-counts before/after.
Together these force $R(0)=R(1)$: \textbf{the target decode must be balanced.}
The pure product is 3:1, so no exact CNOT- (or CCNOT-) gadget exists for it; a
bijection cannot map a 3-class onto a 1-class. Randomising $(a,b)$ flips
\emph{which} side is heavy but never the imbalance itself.

The balanced-implies-affine lemma closes the loop: every nonlinear 2-wire decode
is ungateable, so the nonlinearity must come from \textbf{more than the value's
own two wires}---here, a linear carrier pair plus product terms over foreign band
wires. A second, independent reason the pure product fails: even granting a
gadget, its best degree-1 predictor is right on the whole 1-rep side and,
optimising over the three 0-reps, still achieves accuracy $\mathbf{5/6}$---a
degree-1 leak floor of $H\approx0.17$. The linear carrier symmetrises the classes
and removes this floor.

\section{The share-native fold-CG---never reconstruct an operand}

The predecessor ``deferred-mask'' design masked values \emph{between} uses but,
at each gate, momentarily \textbf{un-masked} the operands so a vanilla gate could
read the true value (``peek''), then re-masked. Validation showed this does not
move the diagonal: the peek re-exposes each gate's operands at exactly the
use-points that form the diagonal. Masking hides between uses; the ridge lives
\emph{at} uses.

The product-share CG removes the peek. It applies $v_t \mathrel{\xor}= f(\text{controls})$
by \textbf{folding $f$'s ANF over the operands' full decodes}: each control value
contributes its summand atoms (two carrier literals, the $k$ product-literal
sets, and a constant when the ledger constant / control polarity applies); every
cross-product of one atom per control operand is emitted as \textbf{one
conjunction fragment} written into a random carrier of $v_t$. A single value's
own product term appears only \emph{inside} a fragment's firing condition,
\textbf{never materialised on a wire}. Constants and the gate's complement go to
the ledger constant $c_t$, so the encoding emits no bare $X$. No wire is ever
written except the target's carriers---\textbf{operands stay masked through the
gate}.

\section{The frozen source band}

The mask sources live on a dedicated band appended after the $2n$ carriers. At
the input port each band wire is filled with an unbiased data-dependent bit (a
random weight-$\ge2$ XOR of the data wires; on the zero slice, $\langle\alpha,x
\rangle$) and then \textbf{never written again}. Because the sources are frozen,
every registered product term is \textbf{time-invariant}---invariant under all RG
churn and CG traffic. Nothing is ever flushed except the final unshare strip; a
mask is one gate, not seven.

\section{$k$ versus degree---two different jobs}

$k$ (number of terms) and $\deg$ (literals per term) are independent levers.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Degree hides algebraically.} A degree-$d$ term is not in the
degree-${<}d$ $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ span, so against the exact-span measure a single
degree-$d$ term forces $H=0.5$ for \emph{every} adversary of degree $<d$,
regardless of $k$.
\item \textbf{$k$ hides statistically.} Against a best-approximation adversary
the terms pile up: $k$ stacked degree-2 masks push the best affine readout error
toward $1/2$ as $\tfrac12-2^{-(k+1)}$. Against the exact-span measure, $k>1$ is
\emph{redundant} for the degree it already blocks.
\end{itemize}
A single degree-$d$ mask is a \emph{sparser} perturbation (it flips the value
only $2^{-d}$ of the time), so wider masks are individually \emph{weaker}
statistically even as they are stronger algebraically---the case for a mixed
design (\S\ref{sec:mixed}).

\section{Towers---raising the degree}

To hide from a degree-$(d{-}1)$ adversary you need degree-$d$ masks. The cheapest
realisation is a \textbf{wider conjunction}: $\deg=d$ uses $d$ band-wire literals,
still one MPMCT gate, degree $d$ in the inputs. Each $+1$ of mask degree forces
the adversary from degree $D$ to $D{+}1$, multiplying its regressor count by
${\sim}W$ (the wire count) and its solve by $W^2$: one control literal for us, a
factor ${\sim}W$ for the attacker.

\textbf{Cost of a tower level is $\sim$flat in gate count.} The number of fold
fragments is set by $k$ and the source-gate arity, not the mask degree; widening
only adds control literals to existing fragments. (Measured at $n=16$:
$\deg\text{-}3,k=3$ is $5\,944$ gates vs.\ $\deg\text{-}2,k=3$'s $6\,161$---
slightly \emph{fewer}, as wider atoms hit more literal-contradictions.) The real
price is \textbf{fragment width}: a degree-$d$ mask folded through a $w$-operand
gate yields fragments up to width $d\cdot w$. Degree-3 reaches width 6 (the
frozen-DB $m=6$ cliff), degree-4 width 8 (past it, needing scratch
decomposition). Towers are nearly free to \emph{build}; the bill is downstream
\textbf{mixing difficulty} (\S\ref{sec:mixing}).

\section{The mixed design}\label{sec:mixed}

Because degree and $k$ do different jobs, the production encoding mixes tiers:
$k$ base degree-2 terms (statistical strength; each a strong $1/4$ perturbation)
\emph{plus} $k_{\mathrm{hi}}$ degree-3 tower terms (degree-2 algebraic hiding).
The validated setting is $[2,2,3,3]$---two deg-2 + two deg-3 terms---which kills
the degree-1 \emph{and} degree-2 diagonals with margin (a single deg-3 term
leaves a boundary residual; two do not).

\section{Interaction with mixing---a caveat and a feature}\label{sec:mixing}

The wide mask fragments (width up to ${\sim}15$ after folding through the
256-wire sandwich's CCNOTs) are \textbf{ineligible for frozen-DB re-encoding}
(the narrow $m1$--$m11$ store, \texttt{-{}-db-ctrl-cap 2}). Operationally,
the all-gates 5th-percentile generation (\texttt{Gall=}) is pinned at 0 because
a large fraction of gates can never be re-encoded. \emph{Since} \texttt{512ce31c}
\emph{the reported} \texttt{G=} \emph{and the dose stop are measured over the
targetable population instead, so ``run to generation $N$'' does converge --- it
means $N$ over the DB-eligible material.} Defensively, the same
fact means the encoding \textbf{resists DB-based simplification}, which is a
property one wants of an obfuscation.

genuine triples over \texttt{-{}-deg2-wire-list} (previously capped at pairs).
\item Correctness: library suite $166/166$; endpoint mismatches $0$ at
$n=6,16,128$; masked builds pass the pinned zero-slice contract; no bare $X$.
\end{itemize}
