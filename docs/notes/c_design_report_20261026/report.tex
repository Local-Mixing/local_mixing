\section{Questions}
RC asked three things after the $C$-restructuring experiments (\code{C\_RESTRUCTURING\_EXPERIMENTS\_20261021.md}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item Why does the balanced $C$ (every internal value read exactly twice as a control, write counts balanced at every cut) leak as many internal values after stage~6 as the random $C$, and is there any parameter on which it is better?
\item Is there a way to design $C$ so that fewer of its internal values are exposed, without growing it or losing the pseudorandomness of the function it computes?
\item Test with the latest stage-2 compute (three-mask, modified) and the latest stage~6 (adaptive, v2), on the server.
\end{enumerate}
Short answers: (1) exposure is a per-control-read phenomenon, the balanced $C$ only redistributes the 2,000 reads, and it does win on read-side metrics (gates with both controls exposed are halved) while losing on the count; (2) within g57 circuits of 1,000 gates on 64 wires, no: every read distribution that lowers the count breaks avalanche, because the random $C$ already sits at the minimum number of read values that full diffusion needs; (3) under three-mask\,+\,v2 every $C$ gives 0/0 in the census, and the $C$-blind windowed observers on the stage-6 outputs reproduce exactly the same per-read law, so $C$ design redistributes exposure there as well.

\section{What was measured, and where in the pipeline}
Pipeline per arm: stage~1 sandwich ($C$ on 64 wires, 1,000 g57 gates, plus $D$ and slicing, 2,832 gates) $\to$ stage~2 compute block (either the pinned baseline \code{managed\_tdp}, ${\sim}778$k gates, or the three-mask compute with RC's modifications \code{-{}-random-helpers -{}-unique-cover}, ${\sim}505$k gates) $\to$ stages 3--5 (DB mixing, split, crossing, reduced profile 2,2,2,2,2; ${\sim}5.5$M or ${\sim}3.5$M gates) $\to$ stage~6 adaptive resynthesis, policy \emph{asis} (one seed, keep-smaller) or \emph{v2} (two seeds, keep-smaller-and-not-leakier, moved boundaries, size-scaled budgets). One master seed is shared by every arm, so $D$, the sandwich and all stage seeds are identical; only $C$ (and the stage-2 block) differ.

Measurement families, all on the honest-slice inputs and all re-validated on 8,192 fresh plus structured inputs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Census} ($C$-aware, every cut, every wire): a $C$ birth is \emph{directly} exposed if some wire version equals it or its complement; \emph{affinely} exposed if it is an affine combination of simultaneously live wires. Reported on stage-6 outputs; every stage-5 circuit in this study has 0/0.
\item \textbf{Narrow portfolio} ($C$-blind): nine windowed resynthesis attacks on a circuit (raw, factor, factor\_perm, factor\_shift63, factor\_w250, pairs, payload factor\_w125 / factor\_w250 / delta\_w250), then an observer scores the attacked circuit; counts are deeper $C$ values found at $\geq 2$ locations (R) or once (O). ``Deeper'' excludes the public-quadratic boundary births (60 for the original $C$, 49 balanced, 73 burst).
\item \textbf{$C$-blind adaptive resynthesis} (resilience step): stage~6 with no $C$ information (largest feasible blocks, keep-smaller) run to plateau on an output, census after each round.
\end{itemize}
Three $C$'s: the campaign's random $C$ (geometric read distribution: 336 of 936 internal births never read as a control, 217 read once, 130 twice, 253 hubs with $\geq 3$ reads), RC's balanced $C$ (reproduced byte-exact, sha \code{d0586653\ldots}), and a sticky-target ``burst'' $C$ ($q=0.67$: 738 of 937 births unread, 104 hubs with 8+ reads) built as a model test; its avalanche is broken on purpose, so it is a diagnostic, not a candidate.

\section{Why the balanced $C$ leaks as much, and what it improves}
Reads per gate are fixed at two, so a design can only redistribute 2,000 reads over 936 internal births (plus 64 inputs). The balanced $C$ removes the 253 hubs but also the 553 values read zero or one time, moving everything to the two-read rate. Same census, same driver (RC's adaptive pass 1), affine with direct in parentheses:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
metric & original $C$ & balanced $C$ \\
\midrule
exposed births & 319 (239) & 455 (283) \\
fraction of control reads whose source value is exposed & 0.69 (0.55) & 0.49 (0.30) \\
$C$ gates with both controls exposed (inputs public; floor ${\sim}29$) & 462 (307) & 248 (127) \\
$C$ gates whose increment is exposed (target before and after) & 120 (67) & 241 (96) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

The count $f(k)$ is concave in the reads $k$, so the equal distribution maximises it; $k\,f(k)$ is convex, so the same distribution minimises read-weighted exposure and, approximately, the number of gates whose two multiplicands are both in the clear. Those two objectives are duals; that is the whole story of ``balanced = same leak''. It also explains the AUC 0.5: with every value at the same read count, which ones are exposed is decided downstream by the synthesizer, not by any source feature.

A random 64-wire g57 circuit needs about 1,000 gates, equivalently about 590 read values, before every input bit reaches every output bit (650 gates: dead input/output pairs; 800 gates: SAC bias 0.12; 1,000: 0.03). The random 1,000-gate $C$ sits at that floor. Every design that lowers the exposed count does so by leaving values unread, and every such design that lowers it by more than a few percent loses avalanche. Adding gates to compensate adds births one for one. Within this family, $C$ design redistributes exposure; it does not reduce it.

\subsection{Where the values appear}
Direct copies in the stage-6 outputs (baseline stage 2, asis round 1): original 222 exposed values, 85 at two or more locations, 365 physical copies carrying 2,317 control reads; balanced 328 / 75 / 468 / 2,714; burst 82 / 38 / 162 / 946. No value is ever live at two locations at the same time in any run: copies are sequential re-materialisations in different blocks. Copies per value grow with fanout (original round 4: 5+ values 3.9 copies, 80\% multi-location).

Fire fragments: a fire is 170 stage-2 gates, about 1,200 stage-5 gates after the $7.1\times$ expansion, five 250-gate windows. The 20 product units are emitted in groups sharing the left factor (17--35 stage-2 gates, 120--250 at stage~5), and any group sums to $L\cdot x_v$, so one group in a window already yields $x_v$. That is why the 250-gate factoring finds 4--13 repeated values on stage-5 circuits while the 125-gate window finds about one, and why the finds are spread along a value's lifetime rather than clustered at its consumer fires.

\section{Conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Exposure after resynthesis is driven by control reads of $C$'s values: about 0.3 per read for the affine family under the baseline encoding, about 0.25 per read for the $C$-blind windowed observers on three-mask\,+\,v2 outputs, and essentially zero for values that are never read.
\item The balanced $C$ does not reduce exposure because it cannot: with two reads per gate the design only chooses who absorbs the reads. It maximises the number of exposed values ($+40$--$45\%$) and minimises the read-side quantities (gates with both multiplicands known halved, no exposed value serving more than two gates); which of these matters depends on what the attacker needs to reconstruct $C$.
\item No $C$ design in this family lowers exposure without losing avalanche: the random 1,000-gate $C$ already has the fewest read values compatible with full diffusion on 64 wires. Concentrating reads (bursts, hot sets, preferential reads) cuts the count by up to 60\% in the model and in the measurement, and breaks avalanche in every variant tried.
\item The lever that reduces exposure is the encoding and the synthesizer policy, not $C$: three-mask stage~2 takes the census from 205--312 exposed values per asis round to 2--3, and v2 acceptance to 0. Against $C$-blind windowed observers the compressed outputs remain exposed (100--230 values) for every $C$ and both policies; that is the open problem, and it belongs to stages 2 and 6.
\item Recommendation: keep $C$ random; spend the effort on the stage-6 side (acceptance rules that also measure the windowed-factoring family, or a remix after the last resynthesis, which restores 0/0 on the raw ledger), and on the stage-2 fire structure (interleaving fires so that no factor group fits a 250-gate window).
\end{enumerate}
