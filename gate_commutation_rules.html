Commutation rules for controlled-XOR gates, with firing attribution

How a shot gate moves past a colliding gate while its action splits into exclusive residues — first for mixed-polarity Toffoli gates, then for g57 gates.

July 2026 · prepared from a working session; all identities verified algebraically · rev. 2: shot/colliding terminology, relaxed R2 rule folded into §4

1 · The goal

We work with reversible circuits whose gates are single-target controlled XORs: a gate (t; f) performs x_t ^= f(x), where the control function f reads wires other than t. Two families appear:

  • Conjunction gates (mixed-polarity multi-controlled Toffolis, "MPMCT"): f is an AND of literals, each positive or negative. This includes X (empty conjunction) and CNOT (one literal).
  • g57 gates [t, x, y]: t ^= x ∨ ¬y — the control is an OR of one positive and one negative literal.

A gate fires on an input when its control function evaluates to 1. Borrowing the shooting-game vocabulary: the gate being pushed through the circuit is the shot gate g, and the neighbor it must pass is the colliding gate h. We want rewrite rules that move the shot gate rightward past the colliding gate:

Rule form and conditions

g h  =  h g₁ g₂ ⋯ g_k

(a)  whenever g does not fire, none of the residues g₁…g_k fire;

(b)  whenever g fires, exactly one residue fires.

Condition (b) is the exclusivity rule: the residues account for the shot gate's firing by a case split, with exactly one case true at a time. This is what lets bookkeeping attached to firings (attribution, generation tags) survive the rewrite exactly. Everything in this note keeps exclusivity; relaxations are mentioned only in passing at the end.

2 · Notation

Circuits apply gates left to right. We write g = (a; F) for the shot gate and h = (b; G) for the colliding gate, and when F reads the wire b we factor F = λ_b · S where λ_b is F's literal on b and S the remaining conjunction; likewise G = λ_a · R when G reads a. Diagrams use the standard symbols:

3 · What exclusivity forces

Before writing any rules, conditions (a) and (b) already pin down most of the answer:

  1. Every residue targets the shot gate's target a. On an input where g fires and residue gᵢ is the one that fires, the right side toggles gᵢ's target while the left side toggles a — they must be the same wire.
  2. The residues' firing sets partition g's firing set, translated to the state after h.
  3. A rule exists if and only if the colliding gate does not read a. If it does, pick an input where the shot gate fires and the rest of h's controls hold but its a-literal fails: the left side toggles both a and b, while on the right exactly one single-target gate fires — a contradiction.

So the whole theory is organized by who writes into whose controls:

4 · Part I — conjunction gates

R0 — no collision: swap freely

If neither gate's target feeds the other's controls, the gates commute unchanged (k = 1). Sharing read wires is harmless — only writes into reads collide. This also covers two gates with the same target: toggles on one wire commute.

R1 — the colliding gate writes a control of the shot gate: the ladder

Passing the colliding gate replaces the old value of b by b ⊕ G, so the shot gate's original firing condition becomes (λ_b ⊕ G)·S. Decomposing this region into disjoint conjunctions gives, for G = ℓ₁ℓ₂⋯ℓ_r:

g h = h · (a; ¬λ_b·ℓ₁⋯ℓ_r·S) h fired, b got flipped · (a; λ_b·¬ℓ₁·S) ℓ₁ failed · (a; λ_b·ℓ₁·¬ℓ₂·S) ℓ₁ held, ℓ₂ failed ⋮ · (a; λ_b·ℓ₁⋯ℓ_{r−1}·¬ℓ_r·S) all held but ℓ_r

That is k = r + 1 residues, one per answer to "did the colliding gate fire, and if not, which of its controls failed first?" — exactly one answer is true on any input, which is exclusivity. All residues target a and none reads a, so they pairwise commute; their order is irrelevant. The simplest instances: h = X gives k = 1 (just flip the b-literal's polarity), h = CNOT gives k = 2:

With more controls on h, the ladder structure appears — later rungs carry longer prefixes of h's controls, and each rung drops every control after its first failing one:

Three practical remarks: the ordering of the colliding gate's controls in the ladder is a free choice (any permutation is valid, always r + 1 rungs); k = r + 1 is tight — the firing region is parity-twisted and no fewer disjoint conjunctions cover it; and the cost compounds multiplicatively across successive crossings, so wide-control colliding gates act as walls. If the shot gate's other controls S contradict one of the colliding gate's literals, the ladder collapses to a swap or a single polarity flip.

R2 — the shot gate writes a control of the colliding gate

By the lemma in §3, no rule of the form g h = h g₁…g_k exists here under strict exclusivity. What does exist is the mirror: push the colliding gate leftward past the shot gate, splitting h. With G = λ_a·R and F = f₁⋯f_s:

g h = (b; ¬λ_a·F·R) · (b; λ_a·¬f₁·R) ⋯ (b; λ_a·f₁⋯f_{s−1}·¬f_s·R) · g

Same ladder construction with the roles swapped; conditions (a) and (b) now attribute to the colliding gate's firing as seen on the left side (i.e. after the shot gate). k = s + 1.

There is, however, a cheaper route if one is willing to bend exclusivity slightly. Keep condition (a) — no residue may fire when the shot gate didn't — but allow two residues to fire together when it did. Then the shot gate can pass rightward after all, and with only two residues, whatever the sizes of F and G:

g h = h · g · (b; F·R) the shot gate itself, plus one export gate

The shot gate passes unchanged; the export gate forwards its disturbance to the colliding gate's target, carrying all of F plus all of G except its a-literal (the polarity of λ_a cancels out). Both residues are gated by F, so condition (a) survives untouched. When the shot gate fires, either g alone fires (the rest of h's controls failed) or g and the export fire together (they held) — never more than two, and it is always exactly these two residues, independent of how wide either gate is. This k = 2 is what the exclusive mirror pays s + 1 for. (For a g57 shot gate the export region F∧R is two cubes rather than one, so three residues — see Part II.)

R3 — mutual collision

When each gate writes into the other's controls, no full commutation exists in either direction, in any residue-based form that respects condition (a) — even allowing both gates to split simultaneously, and even under the relaxed multi-fire regime that rescued R2, since the proof uses only condition (a).

Proof sketch (facing CNOTs)

g = CNOT(b→a) fires iff b = 1; h = CNOT(a→b) fires on the left side iff a⊕b = 1. Suppose all h-residues precede all g-residues. On inputs 00 and 11, h did not fire, so by condition (a) no h-residue may fire, and the state never changes — hence every h-residue's control must evaluate to 0 at both 00 and 11. On two wires, a residue targeting a can only read b and vice versa; every candidate control (b, ¬b, a, ¬a, empty) is satisfied at 00 or at 11. So the h-block is empty — and then input 10, where h fired but g didn't, never receives its b-toggle (g-residues are barred there by (a)). Scratch-wire and swapped-target constructions fail by the same argument, since the banned inputs are ones where nothing ever fires and all wires hold original values. A general mutual pair contains this core on the subcube where both side-conditions S and R hold.

But the collision is semi-permeable. Write F = λ_b·S and G = λ_a·R. The deadlock lives only on the region where both S and R hold; everything else can cross. The cleanest form keeps the colliding gate fully intact and splits only the shot gate:

g h = (a; λ_b·S·R) · h · (a; λ_b·S·r₁⋯r_{j−1}·¬rⱼ) j = 1 … |R| stays behind unchanged crossed to the right

The stay-behind residue is exactly the part of the shot gate that the colliding gate can observe — g's firing restricted to {R live}, the only region where flipping a matters to h. Because it still executes before the colliding gate, h fires identically to the left side with no modification at all. The crossed residues cover {g fired, R dead}; on their region the colliding gate never fires, so they read b safely after it. The two parts partition {g fired}, so exclusivity is exact.

Splitting the colliding gate as well yields the maximal crossing: its residues conditioned on ¬S escape all the way left, the shot gate's residues conditioned on ¬R escape all the way right, and an irreducible core — one fattened residue of each — remains in the original order:

This is optimal: on the S = R = 1 subcube the proof above shows no residue of either family can cross, so the locked core is unavoidable. Total cost |S| + |R| + 2 gates. If S and R contain contradictory literals, both core controls are identically false and the pair commutes outright.

5 · Part II — adding g57 gates

The g57 gate [t, x, y] performs t ^= x ∨ ¬y. In the normal form (t; c ⊕ m) — m a conjunction, c a complement bit — it is c = 1, m = ¬x ∧ y: a complemented conjunction, which fires on the complement of a cube. That complement is itself a disjoint union of cubes, and this single observation drives the whole generalization. The shot role and the colliding role want different treatments.

Shot gates: pre-split the g57 into exclusive residues

A g57's firing set x ∨ ¬y splits as the disjoint union {x = 1} ⊔ {x = 0, y = 0}, so the gate factors into two conjunction gates that already satisfy conditions (a) and (b) as its residues — the pre-split is itself a legal rewrite under the exclusivity regime:

After pre-splitting, the shot side of the system contains only conjunction gates and the Part I rules apply verbatim. Often only one piece actually collides — if the colliding gate writes c, the (a; b) piece crosses by R0 for free.

Warning. The functional factorization g57 = X_a · T(¬b,c→a) must not be used under attribution: both factors fire precisely when the g57 does not fire, violating condition (a). The ladder pre-split above is the attribution-preserving factorization.

Colliding gates: keep the g57 intact — its complement bit is one polarity flip

All rules consume the colliding gate only through its firing function G = c_h ⊕ m_h, and the complement bit enters at exactly one point: a colliding g57 fires — and flips its target — on the complement side of its monomial. So wherever a residue carries a literal reading the colliding gate's target, flip that literal's polarity when c_h = 1. Concretely: in R1 the substitution becomes λ_b ↦ λ_b ⊕ c_h ⊕ m_h (same ladder, run over m_h, with the b-literal pre-flipped); in the R3 stay/cross split, the colliding gate's sensitivity wire set R = m_h ∖ {λ_a} is independent of c_h, so the split is unchanged — only the crossed residues' b-literals get the flip.

The concrete g57 × g57 rules

For pure-g57 circuits, with shot gate g = [a,p,q] and colliding gate h = [b,u,v], the counts follow from the pre-split plus the flips. The centerpiece is R3 (pins b = p, a = u shown; any pin combination gives the same shape):

[a,b,q]·[b,a,v] = T(b,v→a) · T(¬b,¬q,v→a) · [b,a,v] · T(¬b,¬v→a) · T(b,¬q,¬v→a) stay: F ∧ v h intact crossed: F ∧ ¬v, b-literal flipped

The four purple residues partition g's firing set {b ∨ ¬q}: two cubes with v (stay), two with ¬v (cross). The full g57 × g57 rule set:

Two structural facts about the pure-g57 world:

  • Pin asymmetry. The control b ∨ ¬q depends on its first pin everywhere but on q only inside the ¬b cube, so collisions on the q-pin are systematically cheaper (4 vs 6). When circuit construction allows a choice, routing high-traffic wires through q-pins halves residue growth.
  • One-way erosion. All residues are conjunction gates; g57s are consumed by commutation, never produced. A shot front through a g57 circuit becomes a mixed-polarity Toffoli front, while stationary gates stay g57. Any implementation needs an MPMCT representation to host the residues.

The mental model in one sentence: a shot gate commutes past a colliding gate by case-splitting on everything the colliding gate could have done to the wires it reads, one exclusive residue per case; the only thing that can never cross is the part of a mutual pair where each gate genuinely observes the other — and that locked core is small, rare-firing, and precisely characterized.
